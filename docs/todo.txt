- NEXT UP (2026-08-14): should the short-participle row appear at all when it
  is the same form as the regular one?  DECISION NOT MADE.

  measured 2026-08-13 against the dev db (15 pt verbs):
    - 1 verb of 15 has a distinct short form -- aceitar (aceitado / aceite).
      the other 14 show the identical form in both rows.
    - the short row scores 8 attempts at 100% correct, vs 93% for the regular
      row above it.  it is a free copy of the previous answer, not a test.
    - 10 of the 15 short rows have NO example sentence at all: the 10 seeded
      verbs.  examples.json holds 60 slots per verb and zero "short" rows --
      the file predates the participle split.  those rows drill blank.
    - 2 of the 5 that do have sentences are wrong, using ter where the row is
      the ser/estar one: ouvir ("Tenho ouvido...") and por ("tinha posto...").
      cause was a missing "short" gloss in examples.json _guidance.persons,
      fixed 2026-08-13, but that only affects verbs added AFTER the fix.

  three options considered:
    (a) status quo -- always show both rows.  no information leaked, but
        asserts a ter/haver vs ser/estar distinction that is vacuous for 14
        verbs, and needs ~12 example sentences written for rows that teach
        nothing.
    (b) show the short row only when the forms differ.  leaks the EXISTENCE of
        a second form (not its content) before the learner answers.  makes the
        row informative every time it appears, drops the backfill to 1 sentence
        (aceitar), and narrows the unverified auxiliary claim above to exactly
        the verbs where it is actually the documented rule.  implementable as a
        display rule in api.verb_forms -- skip short when form_text == the inv
        row's -- so no migration and reversible.
    (c) collapse to ONE participle row accepting either form, revealing the
        other after answering (the existing FormVariant mechanism, as for
        oico/ouco).  no leak at all, but gives up drilling which auxiliary
        takes which, which is the interesting part.

  (b) was recommended; mike wanted to sit with it.  whichever way it goes, the
  backfill mechanism is the same and needs no new code: add "short" slots to
  web/languages/pt/examples.json and restart.  seed_examples() runs every
  startup, looks verbs up by infinitive (seeded or app-added alike), and
  apply_examples() overwrites existing text when the new text differs.

  open linguistic question that may decide it: for ser/ir/ter/poder there may
  be no natural ser/estar sentence at all ("foi sido" is not Portuguese).  if
  the row cannot take a natural sentence, that is evidence it should not exist
  for those verbs -- and it may be why the model reached for ter on ouvir and
  por.
