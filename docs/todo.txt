    IS THERE ONE RULE FOR PICKING? no. researched 2026-08-13; there are four
    different mechanisms and only one is automatable:
      1. falámos / falamos          VERIFIED against the AO90 text itself.
         base IX: "É facultativo assinalar com acento agudo as formas verbais
         de pretérito perfeito do indicativo, do tipo amámos, louvámos, para
         as distinguir das correspondentes formas do presente do indicativo
         (amamos, louvamos), já que o timbre da vogal tónica é aberto naquele
         caso em certas variantes do português."
         optional by statute, but keyed to PRONUNCIATION -- and pt-PT is the
         variant with the open vowel. so for us: always the accented form.
         SYSTEMATIC: every -ar verb, 1st person plural preterite. automatable.
      2. aceitado/aceite, elegido/eleito   double participles. governed by the
         auxiliary (ter/haver -> regular -ado/-ido; ser/estar -> short form).
         a closed class of verbs. NOT VERIFIED HERE -- standard grammar, but i
         did not confirm it from a primary source, so check before relying.
         the drill doesn't model auxiliaries, so: accept both.
      3. oiço / ouço                free variation, both standard pt-PT.
         VERIFIED ABSENT from AO90: grepped the complete text (all 21 bases
         + nota explicativa), zero hits for oiço/ouço and for every classic
         ou/oi pair (doirado/dourado, toiro/touro, cousa/coisa, loiro/louro).
         and it could not be there: AO90's dupla-grafia categories are closed
         and enumerated -- mute consonants (facto/fato, súbdito/súdito) and
         double accentuation (género/gênero, ténis/tênis) -- quantified in the
         nota explicativa at ~0.5% and ~1.27% of the vocabulary. oiço/ouço is
         neither; it's a diphthong alternation within one variety, i.e. two
         FORMS of a word rather than two spellings of one, which is
         lexicography's job, not the statute's.
         the statute's own posture on conjugation variants, for the -iar class:
           "Existem, no entanto, verbos em -iar ... que admitem variantes na
            conjugação: negoceio ou negocio; premeio ou premio"
         -- both admitted, no rule for choosing. so accept-both is the
         statute's approach too, not just our workaround.
      4. traz / traze               register; traze is archaic/literary.
         NOT VERIFIED. prefer the first-listed.
    so the policy that falls out: DISPLAY the first listed form, ACCEPT any
    listed form, with one systematic override (rule 1, prefer -ámos).
    and this is where the model check earns its place -- choosing among VOC
    variants is judgement, but gated by the VOC list it can only pick a
    legitimate option, never invent one.

    WHAT COUNTS AS "CORRECT IN PORTUGAL": for dupla grafia there is no
    normative answer in AO90 -- it makes BOTH spellings legitimate, and its
    nota explicativa only describes where the pronunciation sits. the usable
    normative test is MEMBERSHIP OF THE VOP, portugal's own vocabulary.
    verified 2026-08-13 by probing csl=pt vs csl=br; unlike the inflection
    tables, the LEMMA INVENTORIES do differ (5 of 8 pairs):
        receção  in VOP, recepção NOT      aspeto  in VOP, aspecto NOT
        ator     in VOP, actor    NOT      amnistia in VOP, anistia NOT
        amígdala in VOP, amídala  NOT
        facto AND fato both in VOP (fato is a different word -- a suit)
        setor/sector and subtil/sutil BOTH in VOP -- neither is wrong in pt-PT
    this caught two overreaches in the guidance i had written: it claimed
    "setor" and "subtil (not sutil)" as the pt-PT forms when both members of
    those pairs are in fact valid in portugal. fixed.
    so: to settle a pt-PT spelling, probe the VOP for both candidates rather
    than reasoning from the statute's pronunciation notes.

    IMPORTANT CORRECTION to the note above: the VOC is NOT pinned on variety.
    verified by fetching the same verbs under csl=pt and csl=br -- the
    inflection tables come back IDENTICAL. csl changes the header (VOP vs
    VOLP) and the lemma inventory, not the forms. so the VOC is authoritative
    on ORTHOGRAPHY (it gives veem, the AO90 form) but its paradigms are
    pan-lusophone: they list every form valid anywhere in the CPLP. it tells
    you a form is legitimate; it will not pick the european one for you.
